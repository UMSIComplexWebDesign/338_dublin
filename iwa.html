What is the IWA?

The IWA, Irish Wheelchair Association, is Ireland’s leading organization for people with physical disabilities. They serve over 20,000 members across the nation through 58 different centres.

The IWA was founded in the 1960s by eight Paralympians. During and prior to that time, people with disabilities in Ireland often lived in hospitals, were institutionalized, or never left their houses. IWA offered them a chance to get out and try sports. As IWA grew, they continued to offer an increasing number of services to the community.

You can learn more at the IWA website.

Speaking with Ann

Ann is a power wheelchair user. She is a receptionist at IWA where she has worked for the past 15 years. Originally from Kilkenny, she has lived in Dublin for the past 31 years.

Ann spoke to us about her experiences as a wheelchair user in Dublin. She also spoke briefly about her upbringing, including living in residential care from two to eleven years old. A main focus of our conversation was on issues surrounding transportation for wheelchair users.

IWA Headquarters

Facilities

Sports Hall
Basketball court with wide pathways on all sides to allow wheelchair users — both spectators and players — to easily navigate the space even while a game is happening. The sports hall is also used to play Boccia, a Paralympic sport similar to bocce ball.
Gym
Open to disabled and non-disabled members. It features accessible equipment such as a wider bench for lifting, ergs with removable seats, and weight machines with removable chairs so wheelchair users can roll right up to the machine. There are hand bikes, ski ergs, and a studio for adaptive fitness classes.
Community Centre
One of 58 community centres nationwide. The day program offers arts and crafts, bingo, and baking. Many members attend the day programs five days a week. The Wheelzcafe serves breakfast and lunch to members and staff daily.

Services

A non-exhaustive list of services at IWA Headquarters in Dublin:

  • Assisted Living
  • Disabled Parking Permits
  • Driving School
  • Community Centre
  • Accessible gym, including adapted fitness classes
  • Holidays
  • Sports — Para Athletics, Para Powerlifting, Wheelchair Basketball, Boccia

Class Reflections

Overwhelmingly, the class’s favorite part of the tour was getting to speak with Ann. As one student put it, “She offered a lot of insight and real world knowledge.”

Ideas that stuck with students: the amount of extra time, effort, and money disabled people have to expend in order to travel, socialize, and go to work; how much planning has to go into every outing for someone in a wheelchair; the public transportation restrictions that able-bodied people don’t notice; and the fact that the youngest person in the IWA sports program is five years old.
